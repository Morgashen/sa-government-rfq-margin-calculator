Practical guide for South African suppliers

How to prepare a responsible government tender application

A strong tender response is more than a low price. It shows, with clear evidence, that your business understands the requirement, can deliver it safely and on time, and has priced the full cost of doing so. Use this guide alongside the specific invitation to bid, request for quotation (RFQ), specifications, forms and every addendum.

A practical bid-preparation workflow

  1. Confirm the opportunity and the rules. Save the original notice and all tender-pack files. Record the reference number, buyer contact, closing date and time, delivery method, briefing requirements and question deadline. Check the buyer's official channel for addenda right up to closing; an amended document can replace earlier instructions.
  2. Make a bid / no-bid decision. Check that the opportunity fits your business, that you meet mandatory eligibility conditions, and that your team can meet the specification, delivery location, quantities, service levels and contract period. Decline early if a mandatory condition cannot be met. Do not rely on a hoped-for waiver.
  3. Turn the specification into an evidence matrix. List each requirement, where your response addresses it, and the supporting proof to attach. Separate mandatory returnables from scored criteria and technical requirements. Use the buyer's exact form and numbering so an evaluator can find the evidence quickly. Ask questions through the stated channel when requirements conflict or are unclear.
  4. Build a deliverable and complete price. Get current written supplier quotes and confirm stock, quote validity, lead times, delivery, installation, packaging, insurance, travel, subcontracting and warranty costs. Check the required unit of measure and total quantities. Account for cash-flow timing and realistic price validity; don't assume that payment will arrive before supplier or payroll costs fall due.
  5. Prepare and independently check the response. Complete every requested field, declaration and signature. Confirm that named signatories have authority, that certificates and claims are current, and that any joint-venture or subcontractor commitments are documented. Ask someone other than the preparer to check arithmetic, consistency, attachments and compliance against the returnables list.
  6. Submit early and retain proof. Follow the required portal, email, physical-envelope or other delivery method exactly. Check file formats, size limits, naming rules, page ordering and any encryption instructions. Allow time for upload or delivery problems. Keep a dated copy of the exact submission, receipt or tracking reference, and any clarification correspondence.
  7. After submission, stay professional. Monitor only the official communication channel, respond to lawful clarification requests by their deadline, and keep a record of what was sent. Do not offer gifts, private benefits or undisclosed inducements to influence an evaluation or award.

Final 10-minute review

  • ☐ Correct tender number and latest addenda
  • ☐ Closing date, time and submission route confirmed
  • ☐ Every mandatory form and attachment included
  • ☐ Prices, totals, VAT treatment and validity period checked
  • ☐ Signatures, initials and authority confirmed
  • ☐ Evidence supports every preference or capability claim
  • ☐ Final files open and match the intended version
  • ☐ Submission receipt saved securely

This is a working aid, not a substitute for the buyer's returnables schedule. A tender may require additional steps or documents.

Price for delivery, not just for the quote

Before entering a bid price, map each cost to a line item or a documented overhead allocation. A quote that omits one delivery cost can turn an apparently profitable award into a loss. Avoid double-counting costs already included in a supplier price or another line.

Cost areaQuestions to resolve before pricing
Supply and fulfilmentAre supplier prices current? Who pays freight, packaging, offloading, installation, travel or import charges? Is the quoted stock actually available?
People and operationsHow many staff-hours, shifts, training, supervision, tools and consumables are needed? Are relief cover and employer costs included where relevant?
Contract exposureWhat are the delivery milestones, service levels, warranty, insurance, retention, penalties and performance-security conditions in the contract?
Tax and cash flowDoes the stated price need to include or separately show VAT? When are supplier, payroll and tax payments due compared with expected buyer payment terms?
Contingency and marginWhat realistic risks could change cost or timing? Is contingency based on a reasoned estimate rather than an arbitrary buffer, and does the remaining margin justify the risk?

The calculator's figures are estimates, not accounting or tax advice. Tender pricing instructions and the supplier's actual tax status take precedence; have material or uncertain tax treatment checked with SARS or a qualified adviser.

Safe work and responsible delivery

For site, construction, transport, maintenance or other hands-on work, safety planning is part of delivery capacity. Identify the hazards and controls relevant to the actual scope before pricing or mobilising.

  • Review the buyer's site rules, access controls, inductions, permits and required safety documentation before the start date.
  • Assign competent supervisors; brief workers on task hazards, emergency arrangements and the procedure for stopping unsafe work.
  • Provide and maintain suitable tools and protective equipment, and verify that workers know how to use them.
  • Plan safe transport, lifting, storage, housekeeping and protection of the public, staff and buyer's property.
  • Report incidents, near misses and changed hazards through agreed channels; record corrective actions and do not conceal an incident.
  • Check that subcontractors understand and follow the same site and safety requirements, and clarify who coordinates each control.

These are general planning prompts, not a complete safety plan or legal interpretation. Apply the current occupational health and safety requirements and the buyer's site-specific rules; obtain competent professional advice for higher-risk work.

Protect your business from tender fraud

  • Verify notices, amendments, contact details and payment instructions using the procuring institution's independently located official website or switchboard.
  • Treat requests for a fee to "secure" an award, confidential competitor prices, gifts, or personal bank transfers as warning signs. Never pay an unofficial intermediary to influence an evaluation.
  • Use a unique password and multi-factor authentication for supplier, email and tender-portal accounts. Limit access to staff who need it and remove access when roles change.
  • Check the recipient and address before sending company registrations, tax details, identity documents, bank letters or signed tender files. Share only what the official process requires.
  • Keep a protected copy of the tender pack, submitted response, supplier quotes and communications. Do not put banking credentials, passwords or unnecessary personal data in this planner or an exported project file.
  • If a message or payment instruction seems suspicious, pause, verify through a known official channel, preserve the evidence, and report it to the relevant institution or appropriate authorities.

Keep a bid file that supports the full contract lifecycle

Use a controlled folder for the notice, specifications, addenda, clarification questions, supplier quotations, calculations, approvals, final submission and receipt. If awarded, add the signed contract, purchase orders, delivery notes, inspection or acceptance records, invoices, payment follow-ups, warranty items and close-out notes. Restrict access to sensitive information, use versioned file names, and follow your organisation's retention and privacy requirements. Clear records help resolve questions about what was offered, delivered, accepted and paid.

Official starting points and common questions

  • South African eTenders portal — check notices and the instructions published for each opportunity.
  • Central Supplier Database (CSD) — manage supplier registration details and check the current requirements relevant to your bid.
  • South African Revenue Service (SARS) — confirm current tax, VAT and compliance guidance for your circumstances.
  • National Treasury — consult current procurement information and official publications.
Does an active CSD profile guarantee that my bid is compliant?

No. Supplier registration is only one part of readiness. The specific tender can set additional eligibility, evidence, pricing, declaration, briefing and submission requirements. Check each notice and its latest addenda.

Can I reuse the same response for every tender?

Reuse a controlled company profile where information remains accurate, but tailor the technical response, evidence, forms, price, validity and submission method to each opportunity. Remove outdated claims and check every version before sending.

Does the preference-points simulator predict whether I will win?

No. It illustrates an indicative calculation from values you enter. The invitation's evaluation system, eligible specific goals, evidence rules, price basis and evaluation process control the official result; competitor pricing is usually unknown.

Is information entered in this planner sent to the website?

The planner processes tender text and entered pricing in your browser and saves progress in this browser's local storage. Downloaded project files contain the saved information, so protect them if they include commercially sensitive details. External libraries and site analytics/advertising scripts may still make network requests. Use browser controls and avoid entering passwords, banking credentials or other secrets.
